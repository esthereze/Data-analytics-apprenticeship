What You Did Well

This assignment wasn't about finding the "correct" answer. It was about avoiding a common mistake: jumping straight to conclusions.

You came up with 12 different hypotheses, which is exactly what an analyst should do before opening a dataset.

Several of your ideas are especially strong:

How to Think Even Deeper

I want to help you move from possible reasons to testable hypotheses.

For example:

"Bad customer service"

As an analyst, your next question is:

"What data would help me test this?"

Possible evidence might include:

Customer complaints
Product return rates
Customer satisfaction surveys
Loyalty program activity
Repeat customer percentage

See the difference?

An opinion becomes something you can investigate with data.

One Thing You Missed

I was hoping someone would mention seasonality.

For example:

School resumes.
A major holiday ends.
The rainy season affects shopping habits.
People receive salaries at different times of the month.

Sometimes a drop in sales isn't a problem at all—it's a normal seasonal pattern.

This is why analysts compare the current month not only to the previous month but often to the same month in previous years.

Analyst's Golden Rule

Here's a rule I want you to remember throughout this apprenticeship:

Correlation is not causation.

If sales dropped after a new competitor opened, that doesn't automatically mean the competitor caused the decline. We look for evidence before drawing conclusions.
